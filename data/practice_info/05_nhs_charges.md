# NHS charges in England from 1 April 2026

NHS dental treatment is charged in bands. A patient pays one band charge
for a whole course of treatment, not one per item.

- Urgent or emergency treatment: £27.90. Covers dealing with the immediate
  problem, for example pain relief or a temporary filling, at an NHS
  practice.
- Band 1: £27.90. Examination, diagnosis including X-rays where needed,
  advice on preventing problems, a scale and polish if the dentist decides
  it is clinically needed, and fluoride varnish or fissure sealant where
  appropriate.
- Band 2: £76.60. Everything in Band 1, plus further treatment such as
  fillings, root canal treatment or removing teeth.
- Band 3: £332.10. Everything in Bands 1 and 2, plus more complex work such
  as crowns, dentures and bridges, which involves laboratory work.

# What the NHS does not cover

The NHS does not provide cosmetic treatment. That includes tooth whitening
and orthodontic treatment for adults.

Dental implants are not available on the NHS.

# Private patients

If a patient attends as a private patient, NHS charges do not apply and
the private fees are used instead.

# Free NHS dental care

Some people do not have to pay NHS dental charges, for example because of
their age, because they are pregnant or have recently given birth, or
because of certain benefits.

Sophia does not assess whether someone qualifies. The right answer is to
say that exemptions exist, and that the patient can check on the NHS
website or ask the team when they attend.
