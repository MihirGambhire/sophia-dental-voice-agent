# Cancelling an appointment

Please give at least 24 hours notice. Appointments can be cancelled by
phoning 01925 630221 between 8am and 6pm, or by email.

Cancelling with less than 24 hours notice is recorded as a Short Notice
Cancellation.

# Missing an appointment

Not turning up without telling the practice is recorded as a Failure to
Attend.

When that happens the practice normally contacts the patient, all future
appointments are cancelled, and the patient may need to start a new course
of treatment, which can mean further charges.

# The limits, counted over a rolling 24 months

Failures to attend:

- An existing patient who fails to attend twice is discharged from the
  practice.
- A new patient who fails to attend once is discharged.
- An existing patient of twelve months or more may be allowed to rebook
  after a first failure to attend.

Short notice cancellations:

- An existing patient who cancels at short notice three times is
  discharged.
- A new patient who cancels at short notice twice is discharged.
- A short notice cancellation may also mean future appointments are
  cancelled.

Combining the two:

- One failure to attend counts as two short notice cancellations.
- So an existing patient with two short notice cancellations and one
  failure to attend inside 24 months is discharged.

# Arriving late

Whether a late patient can still be seen is up to the dentist. Arriving
very late may be recorded as a failure to attend or a short notice
cancellation. Repeated lateness can lead to being discharged.

# NHS courses of treatment

Being repeatedly late or failing to attend can bring an NHS course of
treatment to an early end.

# Appeals and exceptional circumstances

The practice takes extenuating circumstances into account, and a patient
who has been discharged can appeal.

# Deposits

Private examinations booked online take a deposit, and private hygiene
appointments take the full fee. Those payments are not refunded if the
patient fails to attend or cancels with less than 24 hours notice.
