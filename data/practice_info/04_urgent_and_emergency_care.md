# Same day urgent appointments

The practice keeps a limited number of urgent appointments each day. To ask
for one, ring the practice from 8am on the day itself. They are not
released before 8am and they are not bookable in advance.

These appointments are available to existing patients and also to people
who do not have a regular dentist.

An urgent appointment deals with the immediate problem, for example pain
relief or a temporary filling. Permanent treatment may need a separate
appointment later.

# What it costs

On the NHS, an urgent appointment is charged at the urgent band, £27.90.

Privately, an emergency consultation costs £50 for an existing patient who
has been seen for routine treatment within the last three years, and £85
for someone who has never visited or who has not had a routine or new
patient examination in three years.

# When the practice is closed

Patients in Liverpool, Wirral and Cheshire can ring the out of hours
emergency dental number, 0161 476 9651.

NHS 111 can help with urgent dental problems out of hours, and is the right
number for anyone who is not registered with a dentist.

# When to call 999 instead

Some dental problems are medical emergencies and need 999 or A and E, not a
dental appointment. NHS guidance says to get emergency help for:

- severe swelling of the mouth, lips, throat or neck, together with
  difficulty breathing, or difficulty opening one or both eyes
- heavy bleeding from the mouth that will not stop
- a serious injury to the face, mouth, jaw or teeth
- any injury to the head or face that has caused loss of consciousness,
  vomiting or double vision
- signs of a severe allergic reaction, which is a 999 emergency whatever
  the cause

NHS guidance also says not to drive yourself to A and E. Ask someone else
to drive, or call 999 for an ambulance.

# Problems that need a same day appointment

These usually need to be seen the same day, but are not 999 emergencies:

- severe toothache that painkillers are not controlling
- facial swelling with no difficulty breathing or swallowing
- bleeding after an extraction that keeps going
- a tooth knocked out or broken after an injury

# Medication questions

The practice team can advise on dental treatment. Questions about which
painkillers to take, or about other medicines, should go to a pharmacist or
to NHS 111.
